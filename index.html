Our Heritage

From one woman's atelier to a 200-strong family.

VIVA 1960 began as an act of quiet defiance — a small atelier where women could earn a trade at a time when few workshops would hire them. Three generations later, that founding purpose still runs the factory floor.

Marie Nashed with members of her original atelier, the workshop that would become VIVA 1960.
1960

Inspired by the growing women's movement of the era, Marie Nashed opens an atelier in Cairo, employing women as skilled makers rather than casual labor.

1971

Marie, alongside her husband Amin's commercial strategy, ships VIVA's first international order — 60,000 pieces exported to Poland.

1972

VIVA installs the first automatic embroidery machine in the Middle East, a full generation ahead of most regional manufacturers.

1990s–2000s

Marie's son Boulos Massoud and his wife Ivy Gobran take the workshop and formalize it into VIVA1960, a full production factory.

2013

The factory relocates to a purpose-built, state-of-the-art facility in New Cairo, where VIVA still operates today.

2019

VIVA is nominated to GTEX-MENA, a UN International Trade Centre export-readiness program for SMEs.

Today

Mark Boulos leads VIVA forward as Commercial Director, carrying his grandmother's founding purpose into a modernized, increasingly automated factory.

Mark Boulos representing VIVA 1960 at industry events
Technology & Automation

Consistency you can't get from hand-cut, hand-fed production.

VIVA has spent three decades investing ahead of the market — from the Middle East's first automatic embroidery machine in 1972 to today's automated spreading, cutting and template sewing lines. Automation isn't a talking point here; it's how we hold tolerance across a 500,000-unit run.

1

Automated Fabric Spreading

Rolls are laid in precise, tension-controlled layers, eliminating the ply drift and waste of hand-spreading and keeping every marker consistent across the lay.

2

Automatic Fabric Cutting

Computerized marker-making and cutting follow digital patterns exactly, cutting fabric waste and holding every panel to spec before it reaches the sewing line.

3

Automatic & Template Sewing

Programmable template machines stitch pockets, waistbands and repeat details identically, run after run — where hand-guided sewing would drift.

Sustainability & People

Built by women, built to last.

Sixty-five years after Marie Nashed opened her atelier, gender equity and low-waste production are still how VIVA measures a good year — not just a well-shipped one.

Female Empowerment

55% of VIVA's workforce is women, provided equal technical training and paths into leadership.

Near-Zero Waste

Scrap fabric is recycled back into our intimates line, keeping total factory waste to roughly 1%.

Workforce Stability

Over 20% of our team have been with VIVA more than ten years, protecting institutional knowledge on the floor.

Community Training

An in-factory technical training program, run with the Egyptian government, builds local textile skills.
